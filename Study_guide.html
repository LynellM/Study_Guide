Quick Summary

The essay argues that oppressive systems survive not only through force, but through ordinary people participating in lies. The author's solution is personal refusal to support, repeat, sign, or normalize falsehood.

Why It Matters for Audit

It connects directly to internal auditing because auditors depend on truthful reporting, ethical conduct, documentation integrity, and the courage to challenge misleading information.

Author Snapshot

Alexander Solzhenitsyn was a Russian writer known for exposing oppression, censorship, and moral compromise under the Soviet regime. His work emphasizes truth, conscience, and accountability.

Key Ideas to Memorize

  • Lies help sustain corrupt systems.
  • Individuals share responsibility when they repeat or accept falsehoods.
  • The most accessible form of resistance is non-participation in lies.
  • Fear, comfort, and self-preservation often drive unethical behavior.
  • Truth-telling is a form of moral courage.

Internal Auditing Application Scenarios

Scenario 1

Your manager asks you to approve numbers you know are slightly inflated so the department looks better in a review.

Best response

Do not approve inaccurate information. Document the issue, seek clarification, and escalate appropriately if necessary. This aligns with refusing to participate in falsehood.

Scenario 2

An organization hides control weaknesses in a report because leadership wants to avoid scrutiny.

Best response

This is an ethical and control failure. Auditors should report the weakness accurately because concealment increases risk and undermines governance.
